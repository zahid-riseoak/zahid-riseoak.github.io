AI answer engines don’t rank your content — they quote it. To get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Gemini, you have to write pages an AI can lift, understand out of context, and attribute back to you. This post breaks down the four principles RiseOak uses to make MSP content quotable, plus the formats and freshness signals that get pulled into AI answers.

We call this approach the RiseOak Quotable Framework: a way of writing web pages so that any single section can be extracted, understood on its own, and credited to your brand.

![](/images/blog/2026/09/Classic-SEO-and-AI-engine-optimization-img.jpg)

## Classic SEO and AI engine optimization are not the same job

Classic SEO gets a page ranked. [AI engine optimization](/94-of-b2b-buyers-use-ai-search-so-what-is-aeo-doing-for-your-msp/) gets a passage quoted. The difference changes how you write.

|   | **Classic SEO** | **AI Engine Optimization (AEO)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unit that competes** | The whole page | A single passage or section |
| **Goal** | Rank in a list of ten blue links | Get quoted and cited inside one answer |
| **Reader** | A human who clicks through | An LLM that extracts, then a human who reads the answer |
| **Winning move** | Keywords, backlinks, page authority | Clear answers, self-contained sections, named ideas |

Both still matter. But if your content only ranks and never gets quoted, you are invisible in the fastest-growing search surface of 2026.

### Length doesn't matter — usefulness does

Write exactly enough to fully answer the question. No more, no less. A 300-word answer that resolves the query beats a 2,000-word article that buries it. AI engines extract the passage that answers the prompt, so padding only dilutes the part that gets cited. Stop writing when the question is answered.

### Freshness matters a lot

Some structures get pulled into AI answers far more often than plain prose. Lead with one of these whenever the topic allows.

1.  **Listicles.** “7 Signs Your MSP Needs a Co-Managed Model” — numbered, delimited items. Each item is a clean, extractable chunk.
2.  **Data-driven content with hard stats.** Concrete numbers get quoted directly. First-party data is best, because it makes your page the *only* possible source to cite. If your MSP has original benchmark data — average response times, ticket volumes, downtime costs — publish it.
3.  **Comparison content (X versus Y).** “Co-Managed IT vs Fully Outsourced IT” with a comparison table. AI engines drop these straight into “which is better” answers.

## RiseOak Quotable Framework: Proven Best Practices for Creating Content That Gets Quoted by AI Engines

Apply all four to every article. They compound.

### 1\. Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

Lead with the answer.

Both people and LLMs scan in an F-pattern — they read the beginning, skim the middle, and sometimes return to the end. The middle is where attention dies.

Put the takeaway in the first sentence of the page and of every section. State the claim, then support it.

A page that opens with “There are many factors to consider…” gives an AI nothing to quote. A page that opens with the actual answer gives it everything.

### 2\. Atomic Content

Every section must stand on its own.

Take any H2 section, read it in isolation, and it should make complete sense — because that is often all the AI retrieves.

Avoid dangling references like “as mentioned above” or “as we saw earlier.” Repeat the context a reader needs inside the section itself.

The test is simple: copy one H2 section into a blank document, and check that a stranger understands it with zero surrounding text.

### 3\. Entity-Rich Content

AI models understand the world as entities — people, brands, products, places, organizations — and the relationships between them. Make those explicit.

Name entities in full.

Write “a co-managed IT arrangement, where an internal team shares responsibility with an MSP,” not “this setup.”

State relationships plainly: what integrates with what, who serves whom, which tool belongs to which category.

Link concepts to their known category, such as “a PSA platform” or “an RMM tool,” so the model can place them correctly.

### 4\. Simple and Declarative

Short, plain sentences are easier to extract and quote word-for-word.

Use short sentences with one idea each. Keep a clear Subject-Verb-Object order: “An MSP monitors your network around the clock” beats “Around-the-clock network monitoring is among the services provided by an MSP.”

Cut nested clauses, hedging, and passive voice.

Plain declarative facts get quoted; meandering prose gets skipped.

## Name Your Ideas So AI Credits You

Label your frameworks, methods, and terms with a proper name.

When you coin a distinct term for a concept, AI engines attribute that concept — and its definition — back to you.

Give a framework a proper-noun name, then define it in one clean sentence right after.

Reuse the exact term consistently so it becomes the label the AI retrieves.

## Put It to Work

Writing for AI engines is not a trick.

It is disciplined, reader-first writing: answer up front, make every section self-contained, name your entities, and keep sentences plain.

Do that consistently, add freshness and the right format, and your MSP content stops competing for a click and starts getting quoted in the answer itself.
